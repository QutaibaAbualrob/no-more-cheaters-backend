   2. Existing Systems
Before introducing the No More Cheaters system, it is useful to look at how academic integrity is being handled during exams today. Most institutions rely on a small number of well known approaches, especially in online or hybrid settings. These approaches can generally be described as human-supervised exams, automated proctoring software, and systems that use some level of AI support. Each method solves certain problems, but none of them fully addresses the challenges of scalability, fairness, and practicality at the same time.


2.2.1 Human Proctored Exams


Human supervision is the most traditional and widely accepted form of exam monitoring. In this approach, a supervisor is either physically present in the exam room or watches students through a live video feed during the exam.


In practice, the effectiveness of this method depends entirely on the supervisor’s attention. The supervisor is expected to notice suspicious and unusual actions such as looking at notes, using a phone, or communicating with others. One clear advantage is that humans can understand context. For example, a supervisor can usually tell the difference between a student stretching or thinking and a student attempting to cheat. The visible presence of a supervisor can also prevent cheating before it occurs.


However, this approach has clear limitations and gaps. A single supervisor can only monitor a limited number of students at once, which makes it difficult to scale. Human attention also decreases over time due to boredom or distraction. In remote exams, the situation becomes even more complicated, because institutions must hire and coordinate online proctors, which is expensive and difficult to manage, especially for large courses.


2.2.2 Automated Proctoring Software


Automated proctors were the first form of technology used to support the online examination model, which are basically rule-based and require no human supervision.


Normally, it comes with a secure or locked browser that locks access to other applications and sites. Further, it records video of what appears on the student’s computer screen, as well as video and audio of the student using their webcam, making it alert when the student leaves their webcam frame or when they open another tab on their browser.

Tools like Lockdown Browser by Respondus and early versions of Proctor U's Record and Review model this. Some of the benefits of these types of tools include, but are not limited to, the fact that such tools can scale, in the sense that the exam supervisor does not need to monitor each person in real time. Lockdown browsers can help eliminate digital cheating, such as web-based searching.




Yet, the essential drawbacks of these systems are also considerable: the detection logic is usually very simple; therefore, there are plenty of wrong-flagged events. Normal events, such as students adjusting their positions or a pet entering a room, can have the system trigger warnings. It can be very time-consuming and stressful to review large numbers of flagged sessions. In addition, continuous recording of video and audio can easily be perceived as invasive and raises privacy concerns, which again raise anxiety for students during exams.




2.2.3 AI-Assisted Proctoring Tools


More recent proctoring systems attempt to improve on automation using AI techniques, especially computer vision and machine learning, in analyzing exam behaviors.


These systems process webcam video and audio for patterns that may reflect cheating. The common techniques can include tracking where the student is looking, detecting objects like phones or books, identification of multiple faces in the frame, and monitoring audio for conversations. different combinations of these techniques are used in the various platforms such as Proctorio , Examity and Honorlock.


AI assisted systems have the added advantage of reducing the quantity of material that has to be reviewed by supervisors because the systems highlight higher-risk moments rather than all rule violations. In certain cases, they can better distinguish between normal behavior versus suspicious behavior such as briefly looking away versus repeatedly looking down at a desk.




In spite of this, there remain a few major problems to resolve and this is where there is potential to better adapt to each institution and environment despite the progress already made. For example, it must first and foremost be noted that despite the various positive changes made, there still remain a number of key areas which introduce potential problems and this would include the unreliability and varying dependency on light and camera type used, as well as how a student looks. Another problem which is noted is the lack of clarification on how a flagged student is justified, which creates a source of inconvenience on part of the students as well as instructors on a larger scale. Furthermore, high-level AI solutions would inevitably cost a great deal and there is a feeling of loss of ownership over information and a potential future dependency on vendors themselves.


2.2.4 Summary of Gaps


From an examination of these approaches, it is easy to note an identifiable gap . for instance human supervision is fair and equitable, but it is not scalable. Basic automation is scalable but unfair and unaware. there is also advanced intelligent detection, but it is not transparent, affordable, and even engages human control in its implementation. This is why the No More Cheaters project was born out of an identifiable need marked in the gaps above.

Chapter 2 also discussed the basic means by means exam prodding takes place as a means for ensuring integrity. Although this should have been more theoretical, this particular section will seek to summarize said shortcomings and their overall influence upon the creation of No More Cheaters.


3.2.1 Limits of Human Supervision at Scale


The traditional tests conducted under the supervision of a human require complete and unwavering attention by the supervisor in the traditional setting, as well as in the online setting with the help of live videos. However, it is quite obvious that such an affair is quite practical in settings with a smaller number of people to supervise. But as the number increases, it tends to become extremely unwieldy. In the case of online tests, it is quite impossible as it is extremely costly to supervise a number of proctors. In order to overcome this problem, the developers of No More Cheaters have included the facility in the heart of the server itself.


3.2.2 Accuracy and Privacy Trade-offs in Automated Software


The systems based purely on rules for automation attempt to solve this scalability issue but create another. Undemanding rules that look for actions like a face moving off a video or a student switching tabs can often misinterpret student behaviors, like turning their gaze to solve a problem or changing how they are sitting. This adds another step for overseers to scan for false positives. Additionally, automated systems are designed to record video or both video and audio streams at all times. This, to many students, can be quite invasive. The need for more clever systems that understand context while keeping everything but absolutely essential data is evident.


3.2.3 Lack of Transparency and Diminished Human Control in AI Tools


Some third generation advanced AI systems have better alerting capabilities but are also “black boxes,” not providing any real reasons why the alert is being issued. This is not helpful in giving supervisory support confidence in the alert data. In fact, it puts human judgment in the form of a simple “yes/no” to the reasons for an alert. AI, in an academic setting at any rate, is to assist the supervisor, not replace the supervisor. Therefore, the value in this application is the “explainable” alert with interpretive visual aids.




3.2.4 Accessibility and Flexibility Constraints of Commercial Platforms


Moreover, most of the modern systems that can be utilized in proctoring can be very costly, as they tend to follow a model of a pricey SaaS based, proprietary solution. However, a more appropriate solution can be envisaged by a more affordable, flexible, and open solution, which does not restrict smaller institutions due to concerns of vendor lock-in.


3.2.5 Key Takeaways


The existing proctoring solutions are thus creating trade-offs between scalability and cost, accuracy and student privacy, and automation and control. No More Cheaters attempts to minimize the trade-offs between them by appropriate design. The requirements listed in the next section are the proposed solution to this problem, with the intention of creating a balance between them so that supervisors, as well as students, are well protected, while also maintaining academic integrity

3.4.2 System Architecture

The system architecture, therefore is a multi layered design where instructors and administrators interact with a web-based frontend. The requests go through the (REST API) and are then processed by the application server. The recorded videos of the exams are stored in cloud storage; alternatively, they can be stored on premises within the institutions infrastructure, depending on organizational policies and privacy requirements. The videos are analyzed offline by AI modules for quality and to protect against bias. The results of this analysis are also added to the central database, along with metadata from the system. Alerts and notifications are handled by using an external service. such flexibility in deployment includes the capability to run the system entirely at the institution or in a hybrid cloud.
  
3.5 Development Methodology
For this project, iterative and incremental development methodology will be used with an Agile approach as the basis. Agile offers flexibility in terms of development cycles as well as regular review and testing of all parts of the system.
Methodology Used: Agile
Reasons for Choosing Agile:
   * Regular evaluation of AI models
AI modules should be tested, validated and retuned at regular intervals to improve their overall accuracy and quality. By using the Agile iterative development process, it is simple to refine individual AI components with real-time analysis results and performance metrics.
   * Changing system requirements
As you test, analyze and receive feedback from end users, system requirements will likely evolve and require changes to be made to your overall design. Since Agile allows you to make adaptations to the development cycles without requiring a major redesign of your entire system, adopting the Agile methodology is a sensible choice.
   * Incremental development and delivery
System components such as the ability to upload videos, perform offline AI analysis, generate reporting on individual videos with alerts based on results, can all be developed and delivered in small increments. This allows you to detect any potential issues with your components earlier in the development cycle.


   * Regular feedback from stakeholders
Regular and continuous stakeholder feedback from instructors and administrators leads to improved usability, increased accuracy when making decisions, as well as an overall improvement in system reliability.
   * Improved risk management
The use of an Agile approach is also effective at identifying technical and operational risks at early stages of development, particularly for components related to AI performance, data processing and managing system scalability.

Chapter Five. Conclusions and Future Work
   1. Conclusions
The No More Cheaters project has also created a fully working prototype of the post-hoc (non-real-time) AI-assisted exam proctoring solution to fill in the major gaps in the exam proctoring solution. In the new solution, the student streams the exam proctoring sessions, uploads the videos in the cloud backend (Firebase Firestore is used in the real-time solution and cloud storage is used in the video solution), and then later the AI solution will handle the videos uploaded in the backend. The AI solution is built in Python with OpenCV and YOLO.






Key Technical Achievements


Modular Post-Hoc Architecture: The modularity was a success since separating the process beforehand and keeping it distinct from the post-process was effective. It avoids a complex and low-latency streaming process, minimizes network loads, and satisfies students as they know there is no “live surveillance” either. The backend facilitates processing from the upload, storage, queue, processing, and then DB storage of the result video itself.


Supervisor Focused Dashboard: Finally, the React web dashboard offers a step-by-step workflow for the supervisors, showing scheduled exams, the option to run the AI analysis for completed sessions, and the option to examine flagged incidents, including a timestamp, behavior type (“PHONE_DETECTED,” “MULTIPLE_FACES” etc.), a confidence score, and a short snippet of the incident, providing a form of context, minimizing the concern of the “black box” found in other commercial AI tools.




Privacy Aware Design: Here, the videos are stored for a limited period, say 30 days, after which the videos are deleted from the cloud storage, and the structured alert data is stored for reporting purposes, which implements the principle of the “privacy by design” concept as mentioned in our objectives.


Baseline AI Detection Pipeline: Baseline AI Detection Pipeline: The AI module utilizes pre-trained models via PyTorch or TensorFlow. The models used include YOLO for object recognition, OpenCV/MediaPipe for simple face and gaze recognition. The videos will then be processed at a specific frame rate to produce a report written to JSON format showing event to timestamp mappings. The system successfully detects phones, extra faces, long looks away from the screen utilizing specific sample data.




Limitations and Constraints
AI Model Scope and Accuracy: The existing AI is only capable of identifying a number of actions within a range. It is unable to locate the correct gaze vector, detect certain forbidden items such as certain books, and listen to sounds from its environment. The accuracy might depend on the light it is under, its viewing perspective, and the video quality; this results in a number of false readings.


Scalability of Prototype: The cloud backend has a fixed setup in operation. Even though it’s designed to be scalable, it hasn't been tested with hundreds of concurrent uploads or AI jobs. The time taken to process will be linearly scalable based on the video length, but other optimizations like parallel processing or quantization have not been done yet.

Integration: The application is running as standalone. Exams are managed manually. Integration with full SSO and/or LMS solutions such as Moodle has not been performed in this step.
Thus, “the project has successfully demonstrated proof of concept of such a working system, helping supervisors by presenting them with a much smaller list of reviewable video from what would have taken them an hour or so of video review.” As such, it has proved that “a fair, transparent, and cost effective AI-based proctoring system is indeed feasible,” providing it is used “as an academic endeavor rather than an attempt at something commercially presentable.”




   2. Future Work
AI Improvements
   * More varied data can be added for the model and efforts can also be made by adjusting the lighting, poses, and scenes.

   * Add gaze tracking to better monitor eye movement and reduce false alerts from normal head turns.

   * Introduce basic audio checks to detect sustained conversation or help seeking during silent exams.

   * Optimize the AI model with a reduced model weight and dynamic frame sampling to reduce the time taken for computation.

System Performance and Scalability
      * Process videos in parallel by splitting them into segments to speed up analysis.

      * Deploy and test the backend on a scalable cloud platform to ensure it can handle large numbers of concurrent uploads.


Integration and Fairness
         * Integrate with LMS platforms and university login systems for seamless access.

         * Audit the AI for bias and ensure it performs fairly across all student demographics.

         * Enable context aware alerts so exam specific rules can adjust detector sensitivity.

         * Provide clear explanations for AI alerts, helping supervisors understand why events were flagged.

Architecture and System Evolution
            * The back-end will be refactored into modular micro-services that are easier to maintain and also easily scalable..





Overall Goal
               * Make the system ‘smarter, faster, fairer, and more practical’ while at the same time providing a ‘supervising, protecting, and stress-free examination sphere.
